# Eve Count Quantum Systems — Website Visual Style Sheet
*Reusable reference for a full website rebrand*

This stylesheet translates the existing presentation language into practical website guidance: restrained enterprise structure, technical futurism, warm gold accents, and confident forward-looking communication.

---

## 1. Brand Essence and Design Principles

### Brand Essence
Make complex quantum systems feel clear, credible, and ready for strategic action. The website should balance enterprise confidence with a sense of technical possibility.

### Design Principles
1. **Lead with clarity** — state the audience benefit or decision context before introducing technical detail.
2. **Use restraint as a signal of confidence** — prioritize whitespace, hierarchy, and purposeful content over decoration.
3. **Make the future tangible** — use conceptual technical imagery, structured diagrams, and precise language rather than literal science-fiction visuals.
4. **Create a consistent path to action** — every major page should guide visitors toward a relevant next step without overstating capability.
5. **Use gold selectively** — reserve it for emphasis, interaction, highlights, and moments of strategic importance.

## 2. Proposed Colour System

| Colour | Starting Value | Practical Role | Usage Guidance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ink** | `#16181D` | Primary text, navigation, major headings | Default foreground on white or very light surfaces. |
| **Slate** | `#5B616B` | Supporting text, metadata, secondary labels | Use for hierarchy, not for essential small text where contrast is insufficient. |
| **Mist** | `#EEF0F2` | Panels, table headers, quiet section bands | Use to create separation without heavy card treatment. |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | Primary page background, content surfaces | Keep the default canvas light and open. |
| **Warm Gold** | `#B8872A` | Accent rules, icons, emphasis, selected states | Use sparingly; avoid using it as small body text on white. |
| **Luminous Gold** | `#D7AF55` | Primary highlight, button fill, focus moments | Pair with dark text (`#16181D`) for strong legibility. |
| **Gold Wash** | `#F5EBD6` | Callouts, CTA backgrounds, subtle emphasis | Use as a soft tonal field rather than a dominant brand block. |

### Accessibility Baseline
Validate every final combination against WCAG guidance: target at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text, 3:1 for large text and interface graphics, and a clearly visible focus state. Do not rely on gold alone to communicate status or selection.

## 3. Typography System

**Family:** `Inter`, `ui-sans-serif`, `system-ui`, `-apple-system`, `BlinkMacSystemFont`, `"Segoe UI"`, `sans-serif`.  
Use one modern sans-serif family across the site, with weight and size creating hierarchy.

| Role | Desktop | Mobile | Weight & Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Display** | 64 / 72 | 44 / 52 | 700; sparse hero statements and primary page titles. |
| **H1** | 52 / 60 | 36 / 44 | 700; page-level headings. |
| **H2** | 36 / 44 | 28 / 36 | 600; major content sections and pillars. |
| **H3** | 24 / 32 | 22 / 28 | 600; component and subsection headings. |
| **Lead** | 20 / 30 | 18 / 28 | 400–500; introductions and CTA support copy. |
| **Body** | 16 / 24 | 16 / 24 | 400; compact but readable explanatory text. |
| **Label** | 12 / 16 | 12 / 16 | 600; navigation metadata, eyebrow text, and tags. |

## 4. Spacing and Layout

Keep layouts open, left-aligned, and easy to scan. Use a consistent content edge across navigation, hero copy, sections, and footer content.

| Token | Desktop | Mobile | Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Content width** | 1200px maximum | Full width with 20px gutters | Prevent long lines while preserving generous margins. |
| **Outer gutter** | 32–48px | 20px | Align all primary content to one dependable left edge. |
| **Section spacing**| 80–112px | 56–72px | Separate strategic sections without crowding. |
| **Content gap** | 24–40px | 20–28px | Space headings, paragraphs, media, and controls. |
| **Reading width** | 640–720px | 100% | Keep explanatory copy compact and comfortable. |

## 5. UI Components and Separators

- **Navigation:** Quiet, compact, and text-led. Keep the primary action visible without making the header feel promotional. Use Ink for the active route and a thin gold indicator or underline.
- **Hero section:** Use one decisive headline, a concise supporting paragraph, and one primary action. Keep the composition sparse. Pair white space with a subdued technical visual or abstract gold detail.
- **Cards:** Use open content blocks with minimal framing, clear headings, and short descriptions. Prefer a soft Mist surface or a subtle divider over prominent borders and shadows.
- **Buttons:** Use Luminous gold (`#D7AF55`) with dark text (`#16181D`) for the primary action; use a text or outlined treatment for secondary actions. Provide hover, pressed, disabled, and keyboard-focus states with clear contrast.
- **Links:** Use descriptive link labels and a restrained gold or Ink treatment. Show hover and focus through underline, weight, or a visible contrast change.
- **Forms:** Use clear labels, generous field height, concise helper text, and direct error messages. Never communicate validation through colour alone; preserve a visible focus ring.
- **Comparison tables:** Use side-by-side columns, strong row labels, short cells, and a clear reading order. Use Mist for headers and preserve horizontal scroll or stacked rows on small screens.
- **Footer:** Keep it structured and quiet, with consistent navigation groups and a clear closing action. Use Ink or a very dark surface with sufficient contrast and restrained gold accents.
- **CTA section:** Give the closing action a prominent area with one headline, one supporting sentence, and one primary action. Use Gold wash or a dark field; avoid competing actions of equal visual weight.
- **Separators:** Use a 1px solid line in a light muted gray for section boundaries, table rules, and process steps. Keep separators open and understated; avoid decorative frames and heavy borders.

## 6. Imagery and Icon Direction

Visuals should clarify an idea, create atmosphere, or signal technical depth without competing with the content.

| Visual Type | Recommended Direction | Restraint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Technical sketches** | Paper-like diagrams, annotated systems, grid fragments, abstract research marks. | Subdued, spacious, and secondary to the message. |
| **Abstract quantum imagery**| Soft waves, fields, particles, luminous gold traces to suggest connection. | Avoid literal sci-fi scenes, overloaded 3D renders, and visual noise. |
| **Light-based imagery** | Focused light or glow to emphasize discovery and clarity. | Keep glow controlled; gold should be a highlight, not a full-page effect. |
| **Diagrams and icons** | Simple line-based diagrams with clean geometry and consistent stroke weight. | No decorative icon libraries without defined functional roles. |

## 7. Website Page Patterns

- **Landing page:** Sparse hero, concise context, strategic pillars, proof or process explanation, and a clear CTA. Generous whitespace and one dominant visual idea.
- **Strategic overview:** Introduce the challenge, organize the response into numbered points, and lead into detail pages.
- **Pillar detail:** Open with the pillar purpose, then use short sections, diagrams, and an action pathway.
- **Process or assessment:** Show a sequence from context to evaluation to next step, without implying unsupported outcomes. Horizontal steps on desktop, stacked on mobile.
- **Comparison page:** Frame the decision, present side-by-side criteria, and summarize implications.
- **Resource or insight page:** Strong title, concise metadata, readable body width, and related next steps.
- **Contact or assessment CTA:** Prominent action, short invitation, practical expectation-setting, focused form or link.

## 8. Voice and Content Presentation

Write with shared ownership and practical confidence. Use “we” when describing intent, and speak directly to the reader when presenting a next step.

| Voice Quality | Apply It | Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Confident** | Direct sentences and clear recommendations. | Overstatement, hype, or certainty beyond available evidence. |
| **Strategic** | Connect technical context to decisions, priorities, and next steps. | Unexplained technical detail or feature-led lists without context. |
| **Precise** | Define specialist terms; keep each section focused on one idea. | Vague claims such as "modern," "seamless," or "revolutionary". |
| **Forward-looking** | Frame preparation, possibility, and responsible action. | Fear-based language or dramatic claims about the future. |
| **Readable** | Short paragraphs, informative headings, compact lists, meaningful labels. | Dense walls of text, unexplained acronyms, decorative copy. |

### Rules
- **Lead with relevance** — explain why the section matters before adding detail.
- **Use progressive disclosure** — keep the first view concise and make deeper explanation easy to reach.
- **Show evidence responsibly** — include proof points only when approved and supportable.
- **Make actions explicit** — describe what happens next without inventing timelines, outcomes, or service details.

---

## 9. Do and Don't Checklist

### Do
- Maintain a white or very light canvas with strong charcoal/ink text.
- Use gold to direct attention toward interaction and strategic emphasis.
- Preserve generous spacing and a consistent content edge.
- Use numbered points, pillars, process sequences, and comparisons when they improve comprehension.
- Test every responsive layout and colour pairing for accessibility.

### Don't
- Don't turn the site into a presentation replica with slide-like density or decorative framing.
- Don't use gold for long passages, low-contrast text, or every interactive element at once.
- Don't crowd pages with literal technology imagery, excessive gradients, or ornamental icons.
- Don't make unsupported claims, statistics, product promises, or contact details.
- Don't create competing CTAs that make the preferred next step unclear.
